# OlogyWood NIL Readiness — Protect College Sports Act of 2026

**Working legal/product analysis — not formal legal advice. Qualified sports counsel should review any Terms, contract, fee, or operational change before reliance.**

**September 26, 2026 product update:** OlogyWood separately reduced its Fan Club platform fee from 15% to 10% as a business decision. Ology Live remains 15%, and the Fan Club reduction does not change this assessment's distinction between marketplace fees and athlete-agent compensation.

## Verified external legal findings

### Current status as of September 15, 2026

The Protect College Sports Act of 2026 is **not enacted law**. Congress.gov lists S. 4668 as **Introduced**, with the latest recorded action a cloture motion on the motion to proceed presented on August 5, 2026. The Senate Commerce Committee advanced the bill 19–9 and later released revised floor text, but Congress.gov does not show Senate passage, House passage, or enactment. Contemporary reporting on September 14 described a Senate vote as expected but not yet completed.

### Exact five-percent provision

The latest Senate Commerce revised floor text would make it unlawful for an **athlete agent** to “charge a student athlete a fee in connection with an endorsement contract that exceeds 5 percent of the value of the endorsement contract.” It also would prohibit an athlete agent from entering an agency contract whose term extends beyond the athlete’s intercollegiate eligibility.

The cap is therefore **not written as a universal cap on every fee connected to a student athlete or every NIL transaction**. It regulates compensation charged by an athlete agent in connection with an endorsement contract. That distinction is material for OlogyWood’s marketplace, payment-facilitation, subscription, ticketing, and fan-membership fees—but the platform must continue operating as a neutral technology service rather than negotiating or soliciting endorsement contracts on an athlete’s behalf.

### Current federal definitions

Under the current Sports Agent Responsibility and Trust Act, an **agency contract** is an oral or written agreement authorizing a person to negotiate or solicit a professional sports contract or endorsement contract for a student athlete. An **athlete agent** is an individual who enters that agency contract or directly or indirectly recruits or solicits a student athlete to enter one. An **endorsement contract** is an agreement under which a student athlete is employed or receives consideration for use of the athlete’s person, name, image, or likeness to promote a product, service, or event.

### Other revised-bill contract requirements

The revised bill would require athlete-agent contracts and NIL endorsement contracts to be in writing. An endorsement contract would need to identify the services, parties, terms, athlete compensation, termination circumstances for nonperformance, and a statement that validity and payment are not conditioned on enrollment, continued enrollment, or residence except for the specified institution-related exception. Its term could not extend beyond intercollegiate eligibility.

The revised bill would require athlete-agent state registration, certification to the applicable athletic association, and an agency contract before representation. It would create a student-athlete private right of action, allow actual damages and possible fees, and make predispute arbitration agreements and predispute joint/class-action waivers unenforceable for disputes under the Act.

### NIL reporting and state-law effects

The August revised text would require covered NIL agreements over $600 to be reported to the athlete’s institution and applicable intercollegiate athletic association or designated enforcement entity within five days of entering the agreement; compensation not already reported would generally be reported within 30 days of receipt. The revised bill would preempt some conflicting state NIL, transfer, and eligibility laws while preserving state contract, consumer-protection, privacy, fraud, trademark, copyright, criminal, civil-rights, and Uniform Athlete Agent Act rules except where they conflict with the federal framework.

## Executive assessment

> **Bottom line:** OlogyWood has a meaningful NIL product foundation, but it is **not yet ready to represent that its NIL contracts comply with the Protect College Sports Act if the bill becomes law**. The platform is strongest as a neutral technology marketplace—not an athlete agent—and that distinction should be made explicit in every NIL checkout and contract. The proposed 5% cap is aimed at athlete-agent compensation, not automatically at every platform or payment fee.[1] [4] [9]

The most important immediate correction is not a blanket fee reduction. It is to remove unsupported compliance guarantees, distinguish platform service fees from agent fees, collect student-athlete and representative information, and update the NIL contract and analyzer for the proposed written-contract, eligibility, reporting, and dispute protections. OlogyWood should not claim that a contract is “NCAA Compliant” merely because the profile type is Athlete.

## What the 5% cap means for OlogyWood

The revised bill’s language is specific:

> “It is unlawful for an athlete agent to … charge a student athlete a fee in connection with an endorsement contract that exceeds 5 percent of the value of the endorsement contract.” — revised S. 4668, proposed amendment to SPARTA § 3(a)(5).[4]

Under current federal law, an athlete agent is an individual who enters an agency contract or recruits or solicits a student athlete to enter one. An agency contract authorizes a person to negotiate or solicit a professional sports or endorsement contract on behalf of the athlete.[9]

OlogyWood’s current Terms expressly deny an agency relationship, and the platform presently gives athletes self-service profiles, booking requests, contracts, payments, and reporting rather than promising to negotiate deals for them. On that record, the **1%, 10%, 15%, and flat platform charges are not automatically athlete-agent fees under the quoted text**. They are marketplace or service charges. That is a legal classification, however, and substance controls over labels: if staff, an AI tool, or a future representative feature begins negotiating deal terms or soliciting endorsements for an athlete, the risk changes.

The 1% booking fee already falls below the proposed ceiling. The 15% Ology Live fee and 10% Fan Club fee should not be confused with athlete-agent compensation, because the bill does not say every athlete-related platform fee is capped at 5%. The September 26 Fan Club reduction was an owner-authorized business-rate decision, not a conclusion that the proposal caps marketplace fees. The safer control remains a separate ledger and contract presentation for: **athlete compensation**, **OlogyWood platform service fee**, **Stripe processing fee**, and **athlete-agent/representative fee, if any**. Any agent-fee field for a covered endorsement should be hard-capped at 5% if the proposal is enacted or OlogyWood voluntarily adopts that protection earlier.

## Detailed gap analysis

### Contract claims and internal contradictions

The generated NIL engagement contract currently converts `talentType === 'athlete'` into an affirmative “NCAA Compliant” result. The contract then states that the agreement complies with current NCAA policy. That is not a completed compliance determination, and it conflicts with the Terms, which say the template may not reflect current requirements and OlogyWood does not guarantee compliance.

The default disclosure method is “Platform disclosure via Ologywood,” but the Terms state that OlogyWood does not file institutional disclosures for athletes. This should be replaced with a truthful status such as **“Athlete responsible—proof not uploaded”**, **“Submitted by athlete”**, or **“Submitted by authorized representative.”**

The Ology Live session contract says payment is held in escrow, while the Terms say OlogyWood is not an escrow agent and does not hold or control funds. That wording should be reconciled independently of the bill.

### Missing student-athlete intake

OlogyWood knows that a profile is an Athlete, but not whether the person is a current college student athlete, prospective student athlete, professional athlete, former athlete, or another sports creator. That means the platform cannot reliably determine when the proposed protections apply. The profile should collect a private compliance record separate from the public profile: athletic status, institution, NCAA division/association, institution state, eligibility end date, compliance contact, whether the athlete is under 18, and whether an agent or attorney is involved.

### Missing endorsement and agency controls

The athlete booking flow supports appearances, autograph signings, speaking, camps/clinics, and brand endorsements. The rider templates capture useful operational details, but they do not capture a verified agent registration, agency-contract status, agent compensation percentage, agent-fee base, or eligibility-bound contract end date. They also do not reliably require the revised bill’s complete nonperformance termination terms and enrollment/residency nonconditioning statement.

OlogyWood should keep **agency contracts** separate from **NIL endorsement contracts**. The current NIL engagement contract is between the athlete and booker; it should not be described as an agency contract. If agents are ever supported, they need a distinct verified role, registration workflow, separate agreement, and fee cap.

### Reporting readiness

The revised bill would require covered Division I athletes to report NIL agreements above $600 within five days and otherwise-unreported compensation above $600 within 30 days of receipt, including aggregation from the same source over 12 months.[4] OlogyWood currently places disclosure responsibility on the athlete and provides an Ology Live earnings report, but it does not track the agreement deadline, aggregate all NIL compensation, or record submission evidence.

The platform should not claim it files disclosures unless it actually integrates with the required institution or association system. A safe first version is a deadline dashboard, reminders, downloadable disclosure packet, and athlete attestation with timestamp and optional receipt upload.

### Dispute rights

The revised bill would create a private right of action for specified SPARTA violations and make predispute arbitration agreements and predispute joint-action waivers unenforceable for covered disputes.[4] Ology Live’s blanket binding-arbitration clause should therefore receive a statutory-rights carve-out for student-athlete disputes before OlogyWood represents it as future-ready.

## Practical readiness conclusion

OlogyWood is **ahead of many early-stage platforms functionally**: it already has athlete profiles, athlete-specific booking types, reusable contract templates, e-signatures, media-rights controls, payments, NIL earnings reporting, and explicit anti-pay-for-play/disclosure language. Those are valuable building blocks.

OlogyWood is **not yet Act-ready legally or operationally**. The platform lacks structured student-athlete status, representative verification, a separate agent-fee model, eligibility-bound terms, proposed reporting deadlines, and a statutory arbitration carve-out. Most importantly, the current generated contract makes a stronger compliance claim than OlogyWood can verify.

My recommendation is to **keep OlogyWood’s existing 1% booking fee and do not automatically reduce every athlete-related service to 5%** based solely on this proposed bill. Instead, preserve the non-agent marketplace model, make fee categories explicit, cap only a true agent/representative fee if one is ever supported, and update the NIL workflow and contract language before marketing it as compliant with the 2026 proposal.

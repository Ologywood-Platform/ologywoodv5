# OlogyWood EDU Partnership Kit

## Executive one-page pitch

### OlogyWood EDU: From classroom work to professional momentum

Emerging creators often leave school with talent and a portfolio, but without a practical home for the business of creative work. OlogyWood EDU gives students a role-neutral place to build a professional presence, collaborate across disciplines, showcase work, reach audiences, and learn how opportunities and revenue actually move through entertainment.

OlogyWood is built for independent talent—not only traditional recording artists. Students can use the same ecosystem for music, film, performance, visual work, sports and creator projects, events, ticketing, merchandise, books, promotion, and direct fan relationships.

### What students practice

- Building a professional creator profile and portfolio.
- Publishing and presenting work to a real audience.
- Collaborating with talent, venues, and creative partners.
- Promoting a project with clear calls to action.
- Selling eligible creative products and event tickets.
- Understanding audience ownership, email relationships, payments, and platform fees.
- Moving from a school project to an ongoing professional presence.

### What a school gains

A school can pilot a practical creator-business layer without committing to a platform-wide rollout. The recommended first step is one department and one 90-day cohort. The school receives a structured onboarding path, measurable participation signals, and a debrief it can use to decide whether to expand.

The pilot is designed to complement instruction—not replace faculty, career services, legal review, school systems, or institutional policies. OlogyWood does not promise admission, employment, bookings, income, representation, or school endorsement.

### Recommended 90-day pilot

| Phase | Timing | Focus | Evidence to review |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Prepare | Weeks 1–2 | Confirm department sponsor, cohort size, student communication, privacy and policy requirements | Launch checklist and baseline |
| Activate | Weeks 3–4 | Students create profiles, select disciplines, and publish first work or project | Profile completion and first publish rate |
| Practice | Weeks 5–10 | Students collaborate, promote, host or attend events, and test eligible commerce features | Collaboration, audience, event, and project activity |
| Review | Weeks 11–13 | Faculty and OlogyWood review outcomes and student feedback | Retention, quality, safety, and next-step recommendation |

### Success measures

The pilot should be judged on useful activity, not registrations alone:

1. Profile completion and portfolio quality.
2. Percentage of students who publish or present a project.
3. Cross-discipline collaborations initiated.
4. Audience engagement and consent-based fan connections.
5. Event, ticket, product, or promotion activity where appropriate.
6. Student-reported confidence with the business side of creative work.
7. Faculty effort, support needs, safety issues, and continuation intent.

### Pricing guardrails

- Students can begin with OlogyWood's existing Free plan; no EDU payment is required to submit interest or create a free profile.
- Do not publish a discounted EDU price until a school partner and OlogyWood approve the offer in writing.
- If a discount is approved, express it as a percentage of the live plan price and calculate it from the current billing interval at checkout. Do not hard-code concept-art dollar amounts.
- A sensible first commercial proposal is a time-limited pilot using Free access, followed by an optional percentage discount on Starter or Professional for eligible students. Enterprise should remain a separate institutional or advanced-talent conversation until demand is proven.
- School procurement, tax, privacy, accessibility, data-retention, and student-age requirements must be reviewed before an institutional agreement is signed.

### Proposed next conversation

OlogyWood proposes a 30-minute discovery call with one department director or program lead to confirm:

- Department and discipline fit.
- Approximate cohort size and academic calendar.
- Faculty sponsor and student communication channel.
- Required school policies, privacy review, and accessibility expectations.
- Pilot success measures and decision date.

## Discovery-call checklist

- What disciplines and course levels should be included?
- How many students would be realistic for the first cohort?
- Is there a faculty or staff sponsor?
- What student privacy, age, accessibility, procurement, and communications rules apply?
- Are students expected to publish publicly, privately, or by choice?
- What counts as a successful pilot for the department?
- What would make the school expand, pause, or stop after 90 days?

## Internal launch decision log

Before an EDU offer is announced publicly, record the approved:

- Eligible school, department, student, and alumni populations.
- Verification method and manual-review fallback.
- Discount percentage, eligible plans, billing interval, duration, and renewal behavior.
- Whether a school pays centrally or students subscribe individually.
- Refund, cancellation, graduation, and ineligibility rules.
- Privacy, data-retention, accessibility, age, and institutional-review requirements.
- Owner responsible for reviewing applications and revoking ineligible access.
